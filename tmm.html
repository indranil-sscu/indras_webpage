A transfer-matrix (TMM) solver for planar multilayers: dielectric stacks, Bragg mirrors, metal-clad and DBR microcavities, surface-plasmon (Kretschmann) sensors, and absorbing layers described by Lorentz oscillators. It computes reflectance, transmittance and absorptance, angle- and thickness-resolved dispersion maps that show polariton anticrossing, the internal field distribution, and a coupled-oscillator fit that gives the Rabi splitting, Hopfield coefficients and the strong-coupling criterion. Everything runs in your browser.

1 · Structure

What this does. Defines the stack, from the incident medium at the top to the substrate at the bottom, with light coming from the top. Each layer has a thickness and an optical model: a constant n + ik, a dispersive dielectric (Sellmeier: SiO₂, TiO₂, Ta₂O₅, PMMA, …), a Drude metal (mirrors, plasmonics), or a Lorentz-oscillator medium for the excitonic or vibrational transition you want to couple to the cavity, ε(ν) = ε∞ + Σ f ν₀² / (ν₀² − ν² − iγν). Start from a preset and edit. f sets the oscillator strength (the Rabi splitting grows as √f), γ is the FWHM linewidth. Use Repeat block for periodic stacks such as (TiO₂/SiO₂)×8.

Oscillator controls

Light enters from the top (layer 1). Lorentz parameters (ν₀, γ) are in the chosen spectral unit; separate several oscillators with “;”. Drude parameters (Ep, γ) are in eV. The Ag/Au Drude models are approximations that ignore interband absorption below ~400 nm (Ag) and ~520 nm (Au).

2 · Spectrum: R, T, A

What this tells you. Reflectance R, transmittance T and absorptance A = 1 − R − T versus energy, wavelength or angle. In a cavity the bare photon mode is a transmission peak (reflection dip); with an absorber in strong coupling it splits into the lower and upper polaritons, separated by the vacuum Rabi splitting ħΩR at zero detuning. The width of a peak gives the loss rate (Q = E/ΔE). A shows how much matter character each feature has. Switch the x-axis to angle for a surface-plasmon resonance (the reflectance dip in p-polarisation).

3 · Angle-resolved dispersion & polariton analysis

What this tells you. The cavity photon disperses with angle, Ec(θ) = E₀/√(1 − (n₀ sinθ/neff)²), while the molecular transition does not. Where they would cross, strong coupling produces an anticrossing: two branches separated by ħΩR = 2g that never meet. Plot against θ or against the in-plane wave vector k∥ = (E/ħc) n₀ sin θ, the conserved quantity measured in Fourier-plane (back-focal-plane) imaging. Extract & fit follows the two branches and fits the coupled-oscillator model, giving E₀, neff, g, the detuning, the lower polariton's Hopfield coefficients, and the strong-coupling criterion 2g > (γc + γx)/2.

4 · Detuning (thickness) map

What this tells you. Sweeping the thickness of the cavity layer tunes the photon energy through the molecular resonance at fixed angle, as in a wedge-shaped sample. The same anticrossing appears versus thickness. This is the experimentally common way to scan detuning at normal incidence, and it shows which thickness puts the cavity on resonance (δ = 0).

5 · Electric-field profile

What this tells you. The standing-wave intensity |E(z)|² inside the stack, normalised to the incident field, at one energy and angle. On a cavity resonance it shows the field enhancement; for a surface plasmon it shows the evanescent field decaying into the dielectric. Molecules couple in proportion to the field at their position, so the active layer belongs at an antinode. A layer at a node barely couples, which is why the Rabi splitting depends on where the absorber sits and how thick it is. Shading marks the layers.

6 · Theory & references

Transfer matrices (Abelès formalism)

Each homogeneous layer j (thickness dj, complex index ñj = n + ik, permittivity εj = ñj²) supports a forward and a backward plane wave. The tangential components of E and H are continuous at every interface, so the fields at the front and back of a layer are related by a 2 × 2 matrix, and a stack is the ordered product of these matrices.

kx = k₀ n₀ sin θ₀ (conserved),   k₀ = 2π/λ,   kz,j = √(k₀² εj − kx²),   Im kz,j ≥ 0
ηj(s) = kz,j,   ηj(p) = εj / kz,j,   δj = kz,j dj
Mj = [ cos δj   −i sin δj/ηj ;   −i ηj sin δj   cos δj ],   [B, C]ᵀ = M₁M₂ ⋯ MN [1, ηsub]ᵀ
r = (η₀B − C)/(η₀B + C),   t = 2η₀/(η₀B + C),   R = |r|²,   T = Re(ηsub)/Re(η₀) · |t|²,   A = 1 − R − T
θ₀, n₀
angle of incidence and index of the (transparent) incident medium
kx = k∥
in-plane wave vector, the same in every layer (Snell's law)
kz,j
normal wave vector; the branch Im kz ≥ 0 makes waves decay (and evanescent fields beyond the critical angle decay) away from their source
ηj
optical admittance, the ratio of tangential H to tangential E for a forward wave (s: TE, p: TM)
B, C
normalised tangential E and H at the front surface

Sign convention. Time dependence e−iωt, so ñ = n + ik with k ≥ 0 for absorption and Im ε ≥ 0. (Macleod's thin-film texts use e+iωt and ñ = n − ik; R, T and A are identical.) The field profile in section 5 is obtained by propagating [E, H] = [1, ηsub] backwards from the substrate through the same matrices and normalising to the incident amplitude Einc = (η₀E(0) + H(0))/(2η₀). For p-polarisation it shows the tangential component Ex.

Validation (unit tests in the repository): Fresnel coefficients, Brewster's angle, total internal reflection, the quarter-wave Bragg-mirror formula, the Airy function, the Kretschmann surface-plasmon angle, R + T + A = 1, and absorption computed independently from ∫(4πnk/λ)|E|² dz. Interfaces are flat and layers coherent; roughness, anisotropy and incoherent thick substrates are not modelled, and the substrate is semi-infinite.

Material models
Drude: ε(ω) = ε∞ − ωp² / (ω² + iγpω)    Lorentz: ε(ω) = ε∞ + Σj fj ω0j² / (ω0j² − ω² − iγjω)    Sellmeier: n² = 1 + Σ Biλ²/(λ² − Ci)
ħωp = Ep, ħγp
plasma energy and damping of the free electrons (eV)
ω₀, γ, f
transition frequency, FWHM linewidth and dimensionless oscillator strength (f ω₀² is the squared oscillator plasma frequency)
ε∞
background permittivity from all other transitions

Ag: ε∞ = 3.7, Ep = 9.1 eV, γ = 18 meV; Au: ε∞ = 9.5, Ep = 9.0 eV, γ = 70 meV, good for the red and near-infrared. Use tabulated or ellipsometric data for quantitative work below 520 nm (Au) or 400 nm (Ag).

Strong coupling, Rabi splitting and the anticrossing
H = [ Ec(θ)   g ;   g   Ex ],   EUP,LP = ½(Ec + Ex) ± √(g² + ¼(Ec − Ex)²),   ħΩR = 2g
Ec(θ) = E₀ / √(1 − (n₀ sin θ / neff)²),   |X|² = ½[1 + (Ec − Ex)/√(4g² + (Ec − Ex)²)],   |C|² = 1 − |X|²
ħΩR ∝ √(N/V) μ ∝ √f,   filled cavity at zero detuning: ħΩR ≈ E₀ √(f/ε∞),   strong coupling: 2g > (γc + γx)/2
E₀, neff
bare cavity energy at normal incidence and effective index of the mode
Ex
matter (exciton or vibration) energy, dispersionless on the optical scale
|X|², |C|²
Hopfield coefficients: matter and photon fractions of the lower polariton
γc, γx
FWHM linewidths of the empty cavity (computed by replacing each Lorentz layer by √ε∞) and of the molecular transition
N/V, μ
density of emitters in the mode volume and their transition dipole

The collective coupling grows as the square root of the molecular density and oscillator strength, so doubling f increases the splitting by √2 (checked in the unit tests). The splitting must exceed the mean of the two linewidths for the polariton doublet to be resolved; a stricter criterion compares 2g with (γc + γx). For a partially filled or detuned cavity the TMM spectrum, not the estimate E₀√(f/ε∞), is the reliable number.
