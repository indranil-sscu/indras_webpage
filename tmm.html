A transfer-matrix (TMM) solver for planar multilayers: dielectric stacks, Bragg mirrors, metal-clad and DBR microcavities, and absorbing layers described by Lorentz oscillators. It computes reflectance, transmittance and absorptance, angle- and thickness-resolved dispersion maps that show polariton anticrossing, the internal field distribution, and a coupled-oscillator fit that gives the Rabi splitting, Hopfield coefficients and the strong-coupling criterion. 1 · Structure

What this does. Defines the stack, from the incident medium at the top to the substrate at the bottom, light coming from the top. Each layer has a thickness and an optical model: a constant n + ik, a glass (Sellmeier), a Drude metal (mirrors), or a Lorentz-oscillator medium for the excitonic or vibrational transition you want to couple to the cavity, with ε(ν) = ε∞ + Σ f ν₀² / (ν₀² − ν² − iγν). Start from a preset and edit. f sets the oscillator strength (roughly, the Rabi splitting grows as √f·ν₀/nb), γ is the FWHM linewidth.
#LayerModelParametersThickness (nm)
Light enters from the top (layer 1). Lorentz parameters (ν₀, γ) are in the chosen spectral unit; enter several oscillators separated by “;”. Drude parameters (Ep, γ) are in eV. The Ag/Au Drude models are simple approximations and ignore interband absorption below ~400 nm (Ag) and ~520 nm (Au).

2 · Spectrum: R, T, A

What this tells you. Reflectance R, transmittance T and absorptance A = 1 − R − T at a fixed angle. In a cavity the bare photon mode is a transmission peak (reflection dip); with an absorber in strong coupling it splits into the lower and upper polaritons. At zero detuning their separation is the vacuum Rabi splitting ħΩR. The width of a peak gives the loss rate (Q = E/ΔE). A shows where energy is absorbed, i.e. how much matter character each feature has.

3 · Angle-resolved dispersion & polariton analysis

What this tells you. The cavity photon disperses with angle, Ec(θ) = E₀/√(1 − (n₀ sinθ/neff)²), while the molecular transition does not. Where they would cross, strong coupling produces an anticrossing: two branches separated by ħΩR = 2g that never meet. Extract & fit finds the branch positions in the map and fits the two-coupled-oscillator model, which gives the bare cavity energy E₀, effective index neff, coupling g and Rabi splitting. It also reports the detuning δ = E₀ − Ex, the lower polariton's Hopfield coefficients (photon and matter fractions against angle), and checks the strong-coupling criterion 2g > (γc + γx)/2 using the empty-cavity and molecular linewidths.

4 · Detuning (thickness) map

What this tells you. Sweeping the thickness of the cavity layer tunes the photon energy through the molecular resonance at fixed angle, as in a wedge-shaped sample. The same anticrossing appears versus thickness. This is the experimentally common way to scan detuning at normal incidence, and it shows which thickness puts the cavity on resonance (δ = 0).

5 · Electric-field profile

What this tells you. |E|² inside the stack (normalised to the incident field) at one energy and angle. On a cavity resonance it shows the standing wave and the field enhancement. Molecules couple in proportion to the field at their position, so the active layer belongs at an antinode. A layer at a node barely couples, which is why the Rabi splitting depends on where the absorber sits and how thick it is. Shading marks the layers.

Method. Characteristic-matrix (Abelès) formalism for isotropic, homogeneous layers with complex refractive indices, valid at any angle and for both polarisations (H. A. Macleod, Thin-Film Optical Filters; the approach of the Strasbourg T-matrix code described on the Laboratoire des nanostructures resources page). Validated against Fresnel equations, Brewster's angle, the quarter-wave Bragg-mirror formula, the Airy function, energy conservation (R + T + A = 1), and absorption computed independently from the internal field. Interfaces are flat, layers are coherent; roughness, anisotropy and incoherent thick substrates are not modelled. A lossless substrate is treated as semi-infinite.
