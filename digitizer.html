Tips. Put the calibration points far apart, e.g. on the first and last labelled ticks, for the best accuracy; they don't have to be at the origin. X1/X2 must lie on the x-axis and Y1/Y2 on the y-axis. The calibration then corrects for small rotations or skew in scanned figures. Use the magnifier to place points on line centres. Recalibrating updates all existing points.

Method, symbols & references
x = a u + b v + c,   y = d u + e v + f   (affine map from pixel (u, v) to data (x, y))
x(X₁) = x₁,   x(X₂) = x₂,   x constant along Y₁Y₂;   y(Y₁) = y₁,   y(Y₂) = y₂,   y constant along X₁X₂;   log axes: x → log₁₀ x
(u, v)
pixel coordinates in the image (v increases downwards)
X₁, X₂, Y₁, Y₂
the four calibration clicks on the axes, with their typed values x₁, x₂, y₁, y₂
a … f
six coefficients from two 3 × 3 linear solves; they absorb scaling, rotation and shear of scanned figures

Accuracy is set by the click precision (use the loupe) relative to the axis span: one pixel of a 500-pixel axis is 0.2 % of full scale. The six conditions are exact, so the four calibration points are reproduced to rounding error (see the unit tests).
