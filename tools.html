Energy, frequency & wavelength

Photon / per-particle quantities: E = hν = hc/λ = hc·ν̃; molar values multiply by NA; temperature is E/kB. 1 Hartree = 27.211386 eV = 219474.63 cm⁻¹ = 627.5095 kcal/mol.

Theory, symbols & references
E = hν = hc/λ = hc ν̃,   ω = 2πν,   T = E/kB,   Emolar = NAE
h
Planck constant, 6.626 070 15 × 10⁻³⁴ J s (exact)
c
speed of light in vacuum, 299 792 458 m s⁻¹ (exact)
λ
vacuum wavelength
ν, ν̃
frequency (Hz) and wavenumber ν̃ = 1/λ (cm⁻¹)
ω
angular frequency (rad fs⁻¹)
kB, NA
Boltzmann and Avogadro constants (exact)
Eh
Hartree energy, 4.359 744 722 2071 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (CODATA 2018)

All quantities are positive. Wavelengths are vacuum values; wavelengths measured in air are shorter by the factor nair ≈ 1.000 27.

  1. E. Tiesinga, P. J. Mohr, D. B. Newell & B. N. Taylor, “CODATA recommended values of the fundamental physical constants: 2018,” Rev. Mod. Phys. 93, 025010 (2021). doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.93.025010
  2. Bureau International des Poids et Mesures, The International System of Units (SI Brochure), 9th ed. (BIPM, 2019).

Grating pair: GDD = −λ³G / (πc²d² cos³θd), first order, G = perpendicular separation (E. B. Treacy, IEEE J. Quantum Electron. 5, 454 (1969)). Prism pair: leading-order geometric term −4Lλ³(dn/dλ)²/(πc²) plus material GVD × glass path (R. L. Fork et al., Opt. Lett. 9, 150 (1984)); the glass path is adjusted by prism insertion. Values are for a double pass (folded with a roof mirror). Negative GDD compresses positively chirped pulses. The prism expression keeps the leading geometric term only, so use it for design and alignment estimates.

Spectral Jacobian: wavelength ⇄ wavenumber

A spectrum is a density per unit of its axis, so changing axes needs the Jacobian |dλ/dν̃| = λ²/10⁷: I(ν̃) = I(λ)·λ²/10⁷ (λ in nm, ν̃ in cm⁻¹), and conversely I(λ) = I(ν̃)·ν̃²/10⁷. This keeps the integrated intensity unchanged; simply relabelling the x-axis distorts line shapes and band ratios. Header and comment lines are ignored; the data never leave your browser.

Theory, symbols & references
I(ν̃) = I(λ) |dλ/dν̃| = I(λ) · λ²/10⁷   (λ in nm, ν̃ in cm⁻¹)
∫ I(ν̃) dν̃ = ∫ I(λ) dλ,   I(E) = I(λ) · hc/E²
I(λ)
spectral density per unit wavelength
I(ν̃)
spectral density per unit wavenumber
|dλ/dν̃|
Jacobian of the axis change

Relabelling the axis without the Jacobian shifts band maxima and changes relative band areas, most strongly for broad spectra.

  1. J. Mooney & P. Kambhampati, “Get the basics right: Jacobian conversion of wavelength and energy scales for quantitative analysis of emission spectra,” J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 4, 3316 (2013). doi:10.1021/jz401508t
